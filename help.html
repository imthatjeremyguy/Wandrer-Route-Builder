Quick start

From a Wandrer export to a GPX on your bike computer in a few minutes.

  1. Export your Wandrer map. On the Wandrer Big Map, zoom to the area you want, click Download, choose KML/KMZ and Bike. Wandrer emails you the file.
  2. Upload the KMZ. Click Upload KMZ or drag the file onto the page. Your untraveled roads appear in red, and the road network loads on its own.
  3. Set your start. Click Set start, then click the map. Drag the S marker to fine tune it.
  4. Set an end (optional). For a one-way ride, click Set end and click the map. Leave it empty for a loop back to the start. See Point-to-point rides.
  5. Draw an area (optional for some goals). Click Draw and click corners around the roads you want to collect. See drawing and editing.
  6. Pick a Smooth riding setting. Some is a good default. See what the settings mean.
  7. Pick a goal. Collect everything in the area, fit a ride distance, or hit a number of new miles. See goals.
  8. Build the route. The route leaves your start, rides the new roads, and comes back (or finishes at your end point).
  9. Optimize (optional). Lets the solver spend 10 to 60 seconds looking for a better route.
  10. Download GPX and import it into Strava, RideWithGPS or Garmin Connect.

Your start, area and settings are remembered in this browser, so next time you can upload a fresh KMZ and press Build route.

What the map shows

  • Red: new road. Nearly the whole stretch is on your Wandrer untraveled list.
  • Orange: partly new. Only part of the stretch between two intersections is untraveled, at least 15 m of it. The route still rides it.
  • Gray: connector roads the route can use to get between new roads. Turn these off with Show connector roads.
  • Blue: your route.
  • Dark blue: the part already covered while the route plays.
  • Dashed green outline: your area. White dots are its corners; small yellow dots sit between them.

Click any red or orange road to see its name and how much of it is new.

Drawing and editing the area

Only new roads inside the area are collected. The route can still use roads outside it to get there and back.

Drawing

  • Click Draw, then click each corner around the roads you want.
  • Finish by clicking the first corner (yellow), double-clicking, pressing Enter, or clicking Finish shape.
  • Undo a corner with Ctrl+Z, a right-click, Backspace, or Undo corner.
  • Esc cancels drawing.

Editing a finished area

  • Move a corner: drag a white dot.
  • Add a corner: drag one of the small yellow dots between corners. Handy for carving out a section you want to skip.
  • Delete a corner: right-click or double-click it. An area keeps at least 3 corners.
  • Clear removes the area completely.

Any change to the area clears the current route. Press Build route again to get the updated one.

Smooth riding

Every new road in the area gets ridden once. The extra miles come from getting between them: leaving dead ends, working around one-way streets, and linking separate clusters of new road. The shortest way to do that often means turning around in the middle of an intersection and riding back the way you came. That's efficient on paper, but it kills your momentum. This setting decides how much extra distance a smoother ride is worth.

SettingWhat the route doesBest when
Off Shortest total distance, nothing else. U-turns at intersections are common. You want the fewest miles.
Some Replaces a U-turn with a loop back (through the next intersection and around the block) when the loop adds less than about 400 m. Connectors favor straight streets over zig-zags. Most rides. Roughly half the U-turns of Off for a few percent more distance.
Strong Same idea with an allowance of about 900 m per U-turn, a stronger pull toward straight lines, and a small nudge against riding new road twice. Flow matters more than distance. Close to the fewest U-turns possible.

An example

The route finishes part of a street, then later comes back along the rest of it. When it reaches the part it already rode, Off turns around right there. Some and Strong ride on through the finished section and loop back on the next street over: a ladder or figure eight instead of a U-turn.

Good to know

  • On every setting, the route goes straight through an intersection when that's as good as turning, which builds natural ladders and long straight runs.
  • True dead ends (a cul-de-sac with no way through) always need a U-turn. Those count in the stats on every setting.
  • Loops re-ride a little road you've already done this ride. That's expected, and it's why Ridden twice can go up a bit as U-turns go down.
  • The extra cost of turns and U-turns only applies while planning. The miles shown are always real miles.

Skip unpaved roads

On by default, for road bikes. When it's on:

  • Unpaved new roads aren't targets. They don't count toward the goal, and the route won't go out of its way for them. A road counts as unpaved when your Wandrer export marks it that way (dashed red on the map) or OpenStreetMap tags it as gravel, dirt, grass and similar. Farm tracks count as unpaved unless OpenStreetMap says they're paved.
  • Unpaved connectors are a last resort. A gravel stretch counts as five times its length, so the route takes it only when the paved way round is much longer, or there's no other way through.
  • The note under the route says how many unpaved new miles were skipped.

Turn it off on a gravel or hybrid bike to go after every new road, paved or not. OpenStreetMap surface tags aren't complete, so an untagged gravel road can still slip in as paved.

Point-to-point rides

Set an end point when the ride shouldn't finish where it started: riding to work, to a friend's house, or to where someone picks you up.

  • Click Set end, then click the map. Drag the E marker to fine tune it. Clear goes back to a loop.
  • Just getting there? Leave the goal on Everything in the area with no area drawn. You get the ride that picks up the most new road while staying within 1.5 times the direct trip, or the direct ride if nothing new fits.
  • Every goal works the same way, just from start to end instead of back to the start. Max ride distance and Max ride include getting from start to end, so they must be longer than that trip.
  • With no area drawn, new roads are considered when going from start to them to end in a straight line fits the distance: an oval around both points instead of a circle around the start.
  • The readout says mi ride instead of mi loop, and the GPX finishes at the end point.

Goal

The goal decides which new roads go into the route. Every goal finishes back at your start, unless you set an end point.

GoalWhat you getArea
Everything in the areaA loop that rides every new road inside the area.Required
Max ride distanceThe loop that collects the most new road within a total ride distance, for example 25 mi.Optional
New miles targetThe shortest loop that collects at least that many new miles. Add a Max ride to cap the total distance too.Optional

Without an area

  • Max ride distance: for a loop, every new road within half the ride distance of your start is considered, since a loop can't reach farther than that. With an end point, it's the oval described in Point-to-point rides.
  • New miles target: the solver looks around your start until it finds comfortably more new road than you asked for, then picks the best of it.
  • Draw an area when you want to keep the ride to one neighborhood or route around a part of town.

How it chooses

  • New roads are grouped into chunks of a few blocks. The solver first looks for compact areas: good "home bases" with lots of new road around them, each grown outward block by block, so the ride works through one area instead of hopping between distant clusters. It then keeps trying other mixes, swaps and route orders, and keeps the best loop it finds. The search runs longer when there is more new road to choose from: about 12 seconds for a neighborhood, up to 30 seconds with no area drawn over a whole county.
  • Your Smooth riding setting applies throughout, so a smoother setting can mean a little less new road for the same distance.
  • Roads left out show as faded red, and the note under the route sums up what it collected.
  • If a new miles target can't be reached (not enough new road nearby, or not within your Max ride), the note says so and you get the closest it could do.
  • Optimize can shorten the result, but never breaks the goal: it won't go over your distance or drop below your new miles.

Optimize

Optimize keeps searching for the time you pick (10, 20 or 60 seconds). The map updates whenever it finds something better.

  • With a goal (Max ride distance or New miles target), it spends most of its time trying other selections of roads: more home bases, different region sizes, swapping chunks in and out. The rest goes to better routes through the current best selection.
  • With Everything in the area, the roads are fixed, so it only looks for a better route: different pairings at dead ends, directions along streets and riding orders.
  • "Better" follows the goal: more new miles for Max ride distance, a shorter loop for New miles target, and otherwise fewer miles with U-turns, turns and repeats weighed by your Smooth riding setting.
  • Optimize never breaks the goal: it won't go over your distance or drop below your new miles.
  • The note at the end says how many selections and routes it tried and what it gained. Press Stop to end early and keep the best so far.

Playing the route

  • Play / Pause animates a dot along the route in riding order.
  • The slider jumps anywhere along the route, forward or back, paused or playing. Once it's focused, arrow keys step through it.
  • Speed: ½× (80 seconds for the whole route), 1× (40 seconds) or 2× (20 seconds).
  • The readout shows how far along you are, for example "6.2 of 23.1 mi".

Where the roads come from

  • Indiana: road data from OpenStreetMap, rebuilt monthly and hosted with this site, so it loads in seconds on any device. Roads as of shows the data date.
  • Outside Indiana: roads download from public OpenStreetMap servers when you click Get roads. These servers can be slow; finished pieces are saved, so clicking again only fetches what's missing.
  • Refresh roads re-downloads the roads that came from those public servers.
  • Wandrer also builds its maps from OpenStreetMap, so the two match closely. New road matched shows how much of your KMZ lined up, usually 99% or more.

Your data

Your KMZ is read in your browser and never uploaded anywhere. It's saved in this browser only, along with your start, area and settings. Another browser or device starts empty. Clearing site data for this page removes everything.

Known limits

  • Built for Indiana. Fast, ready-made road data is only hosted for Indiana, and that's where the tool is tested. Elsewhere it falls back to public OpenStreetMap servers, which can take many minutes per area or time out (see Where the roads come from).
  • Routes never use sidewalks, crosswalks, alleys, driveways, parking lots, private roads or motorways, even where a shortcut through one looks obvious.
  • One-way streets are always ridden in the legal direction.
  • Stretches with less than 15 m of new road are ignored. They aren't worth a detour.
  • Near the state line, roads on the other side can't be used as connectors yet.
  • The goal searches are strong heuristics, not a guarantee of the single best possible route.
